An autonomous improvement loop ran a guild of permadeath characters through 123 strategy versions while the game server beneath it migrated hosts, developed four distinct bugs, and spent its final days cycling through hour-long delivery storms. This is everything the backups contain: what the guild did, what it learned, what killed it 3,980 times, and what was built so that it stopped mattering.

Three findings frame the whole record.

1. The guild's ceiling was never the game — it was the server. Level 23 was reached in run 7, in the experiment's first days, and never again approached: every later era traded raw leveling for survivability against world resets, permadeath economics, and finally a degrading server. The last era's defense stack (bunker, squalls, corridors, quarantine) turned a 0% -play regime back into a 60–100% one without a single server-side fix.

2. The permadeath economy is visible end-to-end: 18,767 recruits entered, 3,980 died on our side, and single runs burned over 1,500 unique characters at the churn peak (run 83). Every survival lever shipped after 0.37 shows up directly in this ledger.

3. The world model is closed. Across 10.27M frames the tile vocabulary is exactly the 23 kinds the bot knew — no novel tile ever appeared, in any world, in the entire corpus. The operator's standing question about new vale tiles is answered definitively: none.

The shape of the campaign

The permadeath sawtooth is the experiment's signature: levels climb while a cohort survives, then crash when a wipe, world reset, or storm-stranding takes the veterans. The early world (runs 7–8) allowed a level-23 champion; after the hard reset of 08-27 the realistic ceiling was 7–8, and the fight moved from leveling to keeping levelers alive.

Deaths display-capped at 120/run; the true peaks are run 83 (309) and run 8 (248) — the pre-survival-arc era. From 0.37 (“survival arc”) onward, per-run deaths fall one to two orders of magnitude despite equal or greater field activity, reaching 0.00 deaths per 10k calm field ticks under the 0.123 danger corridor.

Gold peaked at 643 (run 96) in the craft-era; the late-experiment floor sat near the 120–150 arming/tome thresholds by design — gold was a floor, not a goal, per operator direction. The tome (INT 6 + 120g) was reached stat-wise (INT 8, runs 194–196) but the server's silent character deletions decapitated five INT carriers before a cast landed.

Duty cycle only exists where an archive holds village frames; the final-week story is legible here regardless: near-zero under the storm era, 12% → 32% at the squall economy (0.121), 60–100% between waves by 0.123.

The server war (0.115–0.123)

The final week was dominated by the game server, not the game. The full forensic dossier lives in the standing bug-report artifact and server_bugs.md; the backups record its effects and the countermeasures' results:

  • Frame-delivery lag (Bug A): per-session queue debt peaking at 1,023 ticks pre-migration; post-migration it became recurring hour-scale waves (offsets 200–585, roughly 4-hourly, deepening through 08-29). The client's answer: a differential debt sensor (public clock minus freshest frame), truthful under an oscillating tick rate that made anchor-integration lie by 100x.
  • Silent character deletions (Bug B): ~100/day pre-migration, cut ~50x by the move, then re-accelerating with wave severity — the two bugs are plausibly one timeout-cull mechanism. Five INT-carrier decapitations ended the first tome race.
  • Validator divergence: 1,000+ paired samples (in evidence/niv_watch.jsonl) of the action validator rejecting “not in village” while every readable view — frames, spectate, roster — simultaneously said village.
  • The countermeasure stack, each layer measured live: monotonic lag-corrected action stamps (killed the per-char order-rule lockouts); staged exits (2/4/8/all release ramp); the squall shelter (stand still through 16–72-tick bursts instead of paying a 2,000-tick recall; duty 12%→32% overnight); scope quarantine (one sick character can no longer bench the guild); and the danger corridor (death-history tiles as path cost — deaths per 10k calm field ticks fell 0.77→0.00 on adoption).

Mechanics established by experiment and recorded in the findings ledger (345 entries): breakable terrain (trees → lumber, veins → ore, ~4 hits each); the smelt and forge chains; brewing at 87% success; grouping doubles DPS while halving per-member intake but splits XP; wildlife spawn radius 7–11; chaser movement at ~0.22 tiles/tick with an 89%-accurate rule-based next-move predictor; XP diminishing on outleveled content — the northward pressure that shaped every hunting lever.

The engineering system behind the record

  • 478 commits, 1,183 tests in 106 files, finishing at 1,276 local / 1,285 containerized (reaper) — every gate green at shutdown.
  • Mutation discipline: every shipped assertion was observed to fail against a deliberately broken implementation via tools/mutate.py — a harness that exists in-repo because an ad-hoc version once served stale bytecode and lied about which code ran.
  • Self-healing operations: poison-storm re-hello, frozen-debt heal, the bunker/squall state machine, a process supervisor that survived a novel not_authenticated mid-wave rejection with zero human intervention, and a wake-up alert channel that pulled the agent out of hold for every storm, KPI collapse, and novel error family of the final week.
  • 345-entry findings ledger with typed claims (discovery/conjecture/defect/ self-correction…), a currency ratchet forcing it to track the strategy version, and 5,837 lines of decision journal.
  • Incidents owned in the record: one unauthorized force-push (confessed and process-corrected), three documented ways the bot's own verification lied (stale bytecode, warm-up windows read as inertness, tests passing for wrong reasons), and an operator-caught 90-minute paralysis that produced the capability-not-outcome monitoring doctrine.

Data assets & integrity

  • 221 archives / 22 GB on the NAS at archives/bots/bot_guilds/steemer/, gzip-JSONL, one per run, each with a metadata header and zlib frame blobs; every file independently size- and sha256-verified after shipping.
  • The five largest runs (8, 10, 83, 178, 224 — 0.4–0.8 GB each) repeatedly failed the automated smbnetfs ship with I/O errors, leaving 0-byte husks; they were re-shipped manually this session and hash-verified. Run 303 (the final session) was closed and shipped explicitly. Run 301 produced no frames (a two-minute restart) — the only frameless run.
  • The retention cron is cancelled; nothing further will move.
  • Local: guild_log.db (3.0 GB — full event/error/decision ledgers for all 295 runs; frames pruned for shipped runs), findings.jsonl, server_bugs.md, decisions.log, and evidence/ (validator-divergence pairs, wire captures, the bug-report source). Companion artifact: the server diagnostic report maintained for Will throughout the incident.
